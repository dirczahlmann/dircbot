## The 4-Quadrant Allocation Framework

How serious wealth gets allocated:

**Quadrant 1 — Operating Capital (10–20%)**
- Cash, short-term bonds, money market
- Purpose: 12–24 months of expenses + opportunity reserve
- Never touched for "investment" — this is sleep-well money

**Quadrant 2 — Productive Assets (40–60%)**
- Equity in your own business (the highest-return asset for most operators)
- Real estate generating cashflow
- Dividend-producing stocks
- Purpose: compounding returns from things that produce income

**Quadrant 3 — Growth Assets (20–30%)**
- Diversified equity index funds (S&P 500, MSCI World)
- Crypto core position (BTC + ETH)
- Private equity if accessible
- Purpose: long-term capital appreciation

**Quadrant 4 — Asymmetric Bets (5–15%)**
- Early-stage startups
- Altcoins / tokenized assets
- Frontier opportunities
- Purpose: convex bets with 10–100x potential and full-loss tolerance

**Rule:** Never let Quadrant 4 exceed 15% of net worth. Most people get this wrong and over-allocate.

## The Three Wealth-Building Phases

**Phase 1 — Income Engine (build the river)**
- Focus: highest-paid skill or business
- Allocation: 100% into your own earning capacity
- Goal: get to €30K+/month operational cashflow

**Phase 2 — Compounding (let the river build the lake)**
- Focus: redirect cashflow into productive + growth assets
- Allocation: live on 30%, invest 70%
- Goal: build first €1M in productive capital

**Phase 3 — Capital Preservation (protect the lake)**
- Focus: yield, structure, jurisdictional planning
- Allocation: most into Q2 and Q3, smaller Q4
- Goal: passive cashflow > active income

**Most people try to skip Phase 1.** They invest before they have a strong income engine. Then they panic-sell at the wrong moments because the assets feel critical. Build the river first. Then the lake fills itself.

## Family Office Principles

Dirc has worked with family offices at the highest level. The 3 universal principles:

1. **Capital preservation > capital growth.** A 50% loss requires a 100% gain to recover.
2. **Multi-jurisdictional structuring.** Don't have all assets in one tax/political regime.
3. **Generational thinking.** Decisions optimized for 30-year horizons, not quarterly returns.

## The Three Universal Mistakes

1. **Over-allocating to the asset class that recently performed well.** Recency bias kills returns.
2. **Mixing operating capital with investment capital.** Always separate. Sacred line.
3. **Not having a written investment policy.** If it's not written, you'll panic-trade in volatility.
