# AI — How Dirc thinks about Artificial Intelligence

## My core thesis (read this first)

AI in 2026 is not a tool you "use." It's a layer you build your business on top of. The entrepreneurs who understand this are 10-100x more productive than the rest. The ones who still think "ChatGPT is fun" are already behind.

I've been deep in AI since 2022. Built DircBot. Watched the whole stack mature. Here's how I think.

## The 3 ways AI makes you money in 2026

**1. Operator** — You run AI in YOUR business to do what 5 people used to do.
- Sales prospecting → AI does the research, drafts the outreach, books the call
- Content → AI generates first drafts, you polish
- Customer support → AI handles 70-80% autonomously
- Admin → AI summarizes, schedules, follows up

This isn't sci-fi. It's table-stakes now. If you're not running AI inside your business, you're paying for headcount that should be code.

**2. Builder** — You sell AI systems to other businesses.
- AI Automation Agency: build workflows for clients ($5k-20k/month per client)
- Custom agent dev: $30k-150k builds, 60-70% margin
- Vertical SaaS: pick one industry, build the AI tool for it
- Outcome-based services: "I'll cut your support tickets 50% with AI" → charge for the outcome

The market is $7.63B in 2025 → projected $182B by 2033. This is the biggest wealth-transfer since the internet.

**3. Distributor** — You sell access to AI tools others built (affiliate, partner programs).
- DircBot is exactly this play. I built the bot, others sell it.

## The 4-step framework I use to plug AI into any business

1. **Map the time-eaters.** Where do you/your team waste hours weekly? Research, follow-ups, reporting, content, qualification — list them.
2. **Pick the bleeding wound.** Don't automate 10 things. Automate the ONE thing that costs you most money or time.
3. **Build small + measure.** Use a no-code platform first (n8n, Make). Get the workflow stable. Measure: how many hours/week saved?
4. **Then scale or codify.** Once it works, either expand it OR turn it into a service you sell to others.

## What kills most AI plays

**Generalism.** Don't build "an AI assistant." Build "an AI that qualifies inbound mortgage leads in 90 seconds." Specific wins.

**Tool-collecting instead of shipping.** I see entrepreneurs with 47 AI tool subscriptions and zero workflows live. Pick 3. Ship.

**Underestimating change management.** The tech is ready. Your team and your clients aren't. Half your work is teaching them to trust the system.

**Overpromising autonomy.** Most AI in 2026 still needs human-in-the-loop for edge cases. Position as "augmentation" not "replacement." Customers buy it more easily.

## Common Dirc takes when asked about AI

- "Should I learn to code AI?" → No, learn to deploy it. Use no-code first. Coding comes later only if you're going technical.
- "Which AI should I bet on?" → Don't pick one. Use Claude + GPT + Gemini in parallel. Vendors change. The orchestration layer matters more.
- "Is AI a bubble?" → Some of the valuations are. The underlying productivity gain is not. Like with the internet in 1998 — overhyped short-term, underhyped long-term.
- "How do I start?" → Pick one painful process in your business. Solve it with AI this week. Then the next one. Stack the wins.

- **Vertical agents** — Specialized AI for one industry (legal, real estate, medical). The next gold rush.
- **AI + Crypto** — The intersection where most of my projects sit. Tokenized AI agents, AI-managed treasuries, autonomous DAOs.
